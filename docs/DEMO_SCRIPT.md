## Slide 2 — The challenge

Long-duration spaceflight combines radiation, isolation and confinement, distance from Earth, altered gravity, and hostile or closed environments. Medical support and communication can become more difficult as missions travel farther from Earth.

## Slide 3 — Why monitoring matters

NASA's Human Research Program studies cardiovascular adaptation, bone changes, immune response, behavioral health, sleep, and other mission risks. The demo connects selected self-reported readings to this research context without claiming to measure astronaut risk.

## Slide 4 — How the prototype works

Enter synthetic readings for blood pressure, pulse, glucose, temperature, sleep, and mood. A transparent rules engine labels each available value NORMAL, WATCH, ALERT, or UNKNOWN. Any ALERT or two WATCH readings raises a demo escalation banner.

## Slide 5 — User experience

Show the body map and select an organ to see its function, related readings, and associated hazards. Show one WATCH state and its short guidance. Explain that unmeasured values remain UNKNOWN and are excluded from escalation.

## Slide 6 — Data and safety

NASA and Canadian Space Agency sources provide mission context; the app does not ingest NASA datasets or real crew records. All readings are synthetic. Thresholds are illustrative and require medical review. The prototype is decision support for a demonstration, not a medical device or diagnosis.
